The Quiet Craft of Reading Slowly

Reading slowly is not a failure of speed; it is a choice about attention. When you slow down, the shape of an argument becomes visible, and the small hinges that hold it together start to creak.

Most of what we read is forgotten within a week. The remedy is not more reading but better retrieval: coming back to a passage, restating it, and connecting it to something you already know.

Good notes are conversations with the text. A highlight says “this matters”; a note says why it matters to you. The second is where understanding lives.

Aside: the same phrase can appear twice. Most of what we read is forgotten within a week, unless we return to it.

Finally, keep your marginalia somewhere you control. A library you own outlives every app that promised to hold it for you.

None of this requires more time, only a different rhythm. Read a section, look away, and say what it argued in a single sentence. If you cannot, the section has not yet become yours.

Over months, those sentences add up to something more useful than a pile of finished books: a record of how your thinking changed, written in your own words, searchable whenever you need it.
